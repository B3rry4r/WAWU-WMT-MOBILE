**Findings.**

1. **defect (D1): the message limits overshoot under parallel sends.** `checkLimits` counts, and the message is written later, with no lock. Repro (`p5.js`, stand-in delay 150 ms): 60 parallel `POST /legal/assistant/{id}/messages` on one fresh conversation: 60 accepted, 60 stored, 60 AI calls, against a limit of 30 an hour. 36 old messages plus 20 parallel: 52 stored, limit 40 per conversation. 25 recent on an earlier intake plus 20 parallel: 44 in the hour, limit 30. Sequential sends stop at exactly 30 (429, `retryAfterSeconds` 3599). The limits exist to cap paid AI calls, so a client can multiply them. The task asked for no overshoot.
2. **defect (D2): `POST {id}/reply` is unlimited and not serialized.** Fifteen parallel replies against one waiting client message: 9 AI calls, 9 assistant messages written, 6 x 409. It is also outside the 30 an hour count, so repeating it while the provider fails costs a paid call each time.
3. **defect (D3, low): Send calls the AI before it claims the intake.** 20 parallel sends open one matter (correct) but `generateBrief` ran 19 times; a failing brief can be re-sent without limit. Claim first, or serialize per intake.

**How the limits are now enforced (D1, D2, D7).** One rule for everything that spends a person's allowance: the check and the write that spends it are ONE transaction under a per-person Postgres advisory lock (`pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtextextended('legal-assistant:' || sub, 0))`, the same pattern as `beneficiary.service.ts`), and the provider is called after the transaction, never inside it. What counts toward the 30 an hour per person: a client message before Send (`LegalIntakeMessage`), a reply asked for again and a brief prepared on Send (new table `LegalAssistantCall`, `kind` `reply` or `brief`, no words in it), and a client message after Send (`LegalChatMessage` of the person's own matters, joined through `LegalRequest.wawuUserId`). What counts toward the 40 per conversation: client messages plus the calls of that intake (checked for a message and a reply, not for the brief, because "conversation full" tells the client to send the brief). Two things the first version did not say: the brief has its own cap (5 tries an hour, `ASSISTANT_BRIEF_ATTEMPTS_PER_HOUR`, `PROVISIONAL(LEGAL-ASSISTANT-BRIEF-ATTEMPTS-PER-HOUR, owner=YOU, why=...)`, also counted in the 30), and a refusal writes nothing and calls nothing. A tap on a quick reply is resolved in the same step, so a double tap is one message and the second is `400 quick_reply_unknown`.

**Why.** Round 3 made `POST /legal/intake/chat/{requestId}` share the hourly allowance. Once a consultant has written, `LegalChatService.answerWaiting` returns for good, so the client's messages there spend no AI call, yet they were still counted: a paying client was refused after 30 an hour to their own consultant (verifier round 3, note 3; main never refuses them).

**What changed (backend, `src/legal-intake`).**
- `legal-assistant-allowance.ts`: new `reserveMatterMessage(wawuUserId, requestId, write)`. In ONE transaction under the person's advisory lock it looks for a consultant line in that thread; if there is one it skips the limit check and writes, otherwise it runs the same `checkLimits` as before and writes. `Spend` no longer has `matter_message` (nothing else reserved it; the type now cannot be misused). `checkLimits` counts a client matter message only if it was NOT written after a consultant had written in its own thread (a `groupBy` of the first consultant line per thread, compared by stamp). Messages written before the consultant still count (they spent an AI call), so the count of AI-answered messages is unchanged by the free ones and a later thread without a consultant keeps its full allowance.
- `legal-chat.service.ts` (`POST /legal/intake/chat/{requestId}`) and `legal-assistant.service.ts` (`sendOnMatter`, the route `POST /legal/assistant/{id}/messages` after Send) both reserve through `reserveMatterMessage`. **Reachability:** the assistant route CAN reach a consultant-joined thread (after Send, `sendOnMatter` writes to the same `LegalChatMessage` thread the consultant writes in, before or after payment), so it needed the same rule and has it.
- **The race.** The decision is made inside the lock, from the rows the transaction sees; a consultant line is permanent once committed, so a message let through free can never be one the assistant answers, and a message counted before the handover is a counted message whether or not the assistant then stands down (`answerWaiting` re-checks). To make "before" and "after" a total order rather than a clock accident, two small things: the client's row is stamped with `new Date()` taken AFTER the lock is held (a request that waited long on the lock would otherwise carry its earlier transaction-start time), and `LegalChatService.sendAsConsultant` (the dashboard reply, `POST /legal/ops/.../chat/{requestId}`, admin route, not in the protected registry, response unchanged) now writes through `writeAsConsultant`, which takes the SAME per-person lock (the matter owner's) and stamps after it. So every client message is entirely before the consultant's line (counted, the AI may have answered) or entirely after it (free), and none can slip past the limit by racing the handover. The consultant's write waits only for that client's own in-flight messages, each a short step.
