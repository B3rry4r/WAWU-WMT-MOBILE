## Goal

R-7: 5 uploads and 1 GB free; 25 uploads and 10 GB with a tick. R-8: free pieces are allowed and count toward the
limit; the old "first upload must be free" rule is removed. M11, M24 and M27 show these.

## Scope

Upload and storage allowance from verification state (numbers per R-7); free content per R-8.

Existing items above the new limit stay; only new uploads are refused (V3 shows the web still works). The web allows 2 GB today (OPS-05 report), so 1 GB is lower for existing free accounts.

## Capability checks

- [ ] An unticked creator is stopped at the 6th upload or past 1 GB; a ticked one at the 26th or past 10 GB.
- [ ] A creator can publish a free piece as their first upload or any other, and it counts toward the limit.
- [ ] A creator already above the new limit keeps every existing piece.

### Findings

- **D1 defect, medium (new text promises something the system cannot do).** The 413 message says "Delete something to make room." (ticked) or "Delete something, or get a verification tick" (free). Deleting a piece frees its upload slot but no storage: no route or job releases `StorageObject` bytes (verified: usage unchanged after delete). A ticked account at 10 GB has no way to get space back at all; a free one can only get a tick. The wording is the builder's, in this task. Unblocked when the message stops promising deletion (or storage release is built and owner-ruled), and the 413 text says only what the system backs.
- **D2 defect, medium (existing accounts harmed).** The 1 GB quota is account-wide, so a free account above 1 GB (for example 1.5 GB, allowed under the web's 2 GB) is refused a KYC ID document, an avatar or any file (verified on the branch: `kyc/id-document` 413, avatar 413; origin/main 200). KYC gates earnings (R-11), so such a creator cannot finish identity checks and cannot free space (D1). R-7 orders the numbers; it does not order blocking KYC documents. Unblocked when the owner rules whether KYC and avatar uploads count toward the 1 GB (or the folders are exempted), recorded in `DECISIONS.md`. How many live accounts are above 1 GB is not known here (G-60: old files are not counted).
- **D3 defect, medium, pre-existing (not introduced by ME-17; same on origin/main).** The storage limit can be exceeded by racing: presign reads the sum, checks, then inserts, not atomically. 8 parallel 400 MB presigns on a free account: 6 succeeded, 2.4 GB reserved against a 1 GB limit; there is no cap on the number except the 20 requests per second throttle and 512 MB each. The task's check "stopped past 1 GB" holds for sequential use only. The upload count has no such race (checked above). Unblocked when the check and the insert are made atomic (a row lock or a conditional insert on the user's sum); likely its own task, since the web has the same behaviour.
- **unclear, low.** The 413 wording changed from "move up a plan" to "get a verification tick" for unticked accounts; it follows R-7 and B1 but the line is not in a ruling. The owner may have a preferred line.
- **differs-for-recorded-reason.** Free accounts 2 GB -> 1 GB and 5 uploads (R-7); ticked accounts 25 uploads and 10 GB (R-7); free pieces allowed first or any and counted (R-8); `reason` object on two refusals and the new `/uploads/allowance` route (additive, R-7 screens). Existing items above the limit stay (task scope).
- **Builder defaults judged (rule 7, WORKFLOW section 10).** "Free accounts drop from 2 GB to 1 GB as R-7 orders": correct as a default; it is a number R-7 gives, not an invented fact. "Free-first removal needs no web change": correct (the API already accepted a paid first piece from #22 and free pieces by `accessType`); but the effects in D2 of the lower limit on existing accounts go beyond what R-7 says and are listed as a finding, not a default.
- **Safe to merge to the live server:** not yet; no data loss and no web regression (244 of 244), but D1 (message) and D2 (KYC blocked for an over-1 GB free account) change what live users can do. Rebuild after the builder fixes D1/D2 and the owner rules on D2; D3 may go as its own task.
