## Findings

1. **medium** One person's burst of requests can deadlock the Hub's connection pool for 60 s, for everyone. Every step that takes the person's lock (`lockStorageQuota`) holds a pooled connection while it waits for it. The create transaction (`src/content-piece/content-piece.service.ts:669-739`, `LOCKED_TX`) and the draft publish transaction (`:~990-1010`) call `throw await this.gate.limitReachedFor(...)` (`:716`, `:1010`) when the slot claim loses; that reads through the global `prisma` (`src/plans/publishing-gate.service.ts:58`), so the lock holder needs a SECOND pooled connection while the waiters hold all of them. Evidence (pg_stat_activity sampled every 2 s): the holder `idle in transaction` right after `UPDATE "CreatorState" SET "slotsUsed"`, 17 `Lock/advisory` waiters for 60 s, then "Unable to start a transaction in the given time" (15 s) and "A query cannot be executed on an expired transaction" (60 s). Repro: `scripts/q2.py 20 8` (a person with 4 of 5 products sends 20 confirms and 8 creates together; logs/q2-20-8.log): confirm 4 of 20 and create 2 of 8 answer 500, slowest 60.3 s, and another person's `GET /users/me` stalled 59.8 s (`20 10` the same: 7 confirms 500, B 59.9 s). `scripts/s1.py 2` with the person at `productsIncluded` 5 (the storm's 21 concurrent requests, 3 creates racing the last slot): round 1 took 60.7 s, 500 on 6 confirms and 6 releases. With `productsIncluded` 500 the same storm passes (1 to 3 s a round). `q2.py 12 6` (18 requests) did not stall; the threshold is about the pool size. Not a bucket wait, but the lead's rule is the pool: one authenticated account freezes every other user. Fix: build the refusal after the transaction (throw a marker, call `limitReachedFor` once the transaction has ended), or read it with `tx`; and bound how many requests of one person wait for the lock at a time (an in-process per-person queue), so waiters do not hold connections.
2. **low** `20261023100000_storage_object_late_sweep` sorts BEFORE two other origin branches' migrations (wmt/WALLET-09 `20261023200000_bank_transfers`, wmt/BILLS-04 `20261023210000_wallet_bills`); it sorts after every other branch and main. Tested: a fresh database with both of those applied first, then ours added: `prisma migrate deploy` applies it fine (exit 0, six nullable columns, no default). So no failure; `migrate dev` would call it out of order. Say it in the merge order if wanted.
3. **low** (decision (c) side effect, by design) A release whose DELETE got no answer (hung bucket) answers 503 after 20.1 s and leaves the row released and uncounted; the file can stay in the bucket until the sweep's first look (row created + 15 min, then a 10-minute tick), so `usedBytes` is below the bucket's bytes for up to about 25 minutes (`scripts/k5.py`; healthy sweep then removes it, 1 of 1). A stale mark taken over by a sweep while the first release then fails leaves the row released (not restored) and answers that release 503; the file is removed by the next sweep (`scripts/d1.py`, mode delete500). Both consistent, nothing lost.
